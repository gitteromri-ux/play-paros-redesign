The Opportunity

Forty rooms.
Zero kitchens.
One obvious move.

Play Paros has never operated its own food or beverage service. Breakfast walks out the door to a neighbour every morning. The rooftop sits empty most of the day. Here is what changes that — and what it's worth.

Today

Guests are sent to a restaurant next door for breakfast. The only listed F&B facility is a beach bar. There is no branded rooftop offer, no in-house dining, no bar programme — on a rooftop with a full harbour and hillside view.

The proof it works anyway

Despite sending guests off-site, Play Paros's outsourced à la carte breakfast already scores 10.0 on Booking.com — the single highest-rated element of the entire stay. The demand for great food is already validated. It just isn't captured under Play's own roof, brand, or margin.

01

Rooftop, reborn

The view is already five-star — harbour, hillside, open sky. Add a branded sunset bar programme: signature cocktails poured at golden hour, a small plates menu, string lighting and music curation. This is the single highest-margin square footage the hotel owns, and today it holds empty dining tables.

Highest-margin real estate on the property · currently unused after breakfast hours
02

Breakfast, brought home

The outsourced breakfast already earns a 10.0 rating — proof the demand and the quality bar both exist. A low-capex, branded in-house breakfast module keeps that spend (and that five-star review driver) inside Play's own P&L instead of walking next door every morning.

Booking.com breakfast score today: 10.0, entirely outsourced
03

Beach bar, upgraded

The only F&B facility currently listed is a beach bar with no menu identity. A proper cocktail and light-bites programme — the exact moment already happening in this photograph — converts an amenity guests stumble into into a reason to book direct.

Only F&B facility currently listed for the property · no branded menu today

Group-wide, F&B is the single largest driver of negative guest sentiment across Brown's Greek resorts — but Play Paros's own numbers point the other way: no onsite kitchen, and still a 10.0 breakfast score. That is not a taste problem. It's an execution gap with the fastest route to incremental EBITDA in the portfolio, because it doesn't require renegotiating a single lease.
